DAY 10 — BAITING AND WATERING HOLE ATTACK SIMULATION

**1. Objective**

To demonstrate how a local honeypot can record visits to a simulated bait link and explain defenses against baiting, watering-hole attacks, and drive-by downloads.

**2. Attacker Perspective**

Baiting uses an enticing physical device or digital offer to persuade someone to take an unsafe action. A watering-hole attack compromises a website frequently visited by a target group. Both approaches may be used to deliver malware or facilitate social engineering.

**3. Defender Perspective**

Users should avoid unknown USB devices, verify download sources, and avoid suspicious links. Organizations should use web filtering, endpoint protection, application control, timely patching, and security-awareness training.

**4. Implementation**

The Python honeypot runs on localhost and records the UTC timestamp, client IP address, requested path, and User-Agent. The log analyzer counts visits and paths and produces a JSON summary.

**5. Results**

The local test generated an HTTP 200 response and recorded one visit to `/bait-download`. The analyzer successfully generated `reports/visit_analysis.json` with one total visit and one unique path.

**6. Defensive Recommendations**

- Disable unnecessary AutoRun and restrict removable media.
- Use endpoint protection and application allowlisting.
- Filter suspicious websites and downloads.
- Keep browsers, operating systems, and applications patched.
- Verify links and downloads through trusted sources.
- Monitor unusual activity while respecting privacy and data-retention requirements.
- Train users to report suspicious devices, links, and websites.

**7. Limitations**

The honeypot records basic HTTP request metadata. A recorded visit does not establish malicious intent or prove that a device is compromised. The local demonstration does not emulate browser exploitation, malware delivery, or a compromised external website.

**8. Ethical Scope**

The server was tested on localhost using a manually initiated request. No external users or systems were targeted, and no files were downloaded or executed.
