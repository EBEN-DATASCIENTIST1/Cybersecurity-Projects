DAY 9 — SOCIAL MEDIA IMPERSONATION AND FAKE PROFILE DETECTION

**1. Objective**

To build a Python program that assigns heuristic risk scores to social media profiles using account age, follower-to-following ratio, profile-picture availability, post count, and biography characteristics.

**2. Attacker Perspective**

Fake profiles may be used to impersonate individuals, build trust, spread scams, or support social-engineering attacks. Indicators such as a recently created account, unusual following patterns, and incomplete profile information may warrant further review.

**3. Defender Perspective**

Users and security teams should evaluate multiple indicators, verify suspicious accounts through trusted channels, enable multi-factor authentication, and report suspected impersonation through the platform's official process.

**4. Implementation**

The Python program reads five fictional profiles from `data/sample_profiles.json`, calculates scores from 0 to 100, assigns risk categories, and saves results to `reports/profile_analysis.json`.

**5. Results**

- Sample A: 100 — HIGH
- Sample B: 0 — LOW
- Sample C: 80 — HIGH
- Sample D: 20 — LOW
- Sample E: 25 — LOW

The results demonstrate how the selected heuristics influence each profile's score.

**6. Limitations**

The scoring rules are simple heuristics and have not been validated against a labeled dataset. A low score does not prove that an account is legitimate, and a high score does not prove impersonation. Some legitimate accounts may share these characteristics.

**7. Recommendations**

- Verify identity using trusted communication channels.
- Review multiple indicators rather than relying on one signal.
- Avoid clicking suspicious links or sharing credentials.
- Report suspected impersonation to the relevant platform.
- Minimize the collection and retention of personal information.
- Validate scoring rules against an appropriate, consented dataset before operational use.

**8. Ethical Scope**

This demonstration uses fictional profile data. It does not scrape social media, contact account owners, or make automated accusations against real people.
