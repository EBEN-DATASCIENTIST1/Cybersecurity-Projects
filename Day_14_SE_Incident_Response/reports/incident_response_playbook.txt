# DAY 14 — SOCIAL ENGINEERING INCIDENT RESPONSE PLAYBOOK

## Objective

Provide a structured response to suspected social engineering incidents while preserving evidence and minimizing damage.

## 1. Preparation

- Maintain an incident response contact list.
- Establish procedures for reporting phishing emails and suspicious devices.
- Ensure security logs are retained according to policy.
- Define roles and escalation criteria.

## 2. Identification

- Review suspicious messages, login failures, account alerts, and email-rule changes.
- Confirm the affected user, system, and incident type.
- Record timestamps, relevant logs, and initial findings.
- Assign an incident identifier and severity.

## 3. Containment

- Contact the authorized security or incident response team.
- If compromise is suspected, consider account restrictions, credential resets, or session revocation through approved procedures.
- Quarantine confirmed malicious emails using authorized security tools.
- Isolate affected devices only when justified by the incident and organizational policy.

## 4. Eradication

- Remove unauthorized email rules and malicious messages.
- Investigate suspicious account activity.
- Remove identified malicious files or software using approved procedures.
- Address the underlying cause, such as compromised credentials or unpatched software.

## 5. Recovery

- Restore affected accounts and systems safely.
- Verify security controls and account settings.
- Monitor for repeated suspicious activity.
- Confirm recovery with the responsible security team.

## 6. Lessons Learned

- Document the timeline, impact, actions, and outcome.
- Identify weaknesses in technical controls or user awareness.
- Update detection rules and response procedures.
- Provide additional training where appropriate.

## Evidence and Communication

Preserve relevant email headers, security logs, timestamps, and incident notes. Restrict access to sensitive evidence. Notify affected users and relevant stakeholders through approved communication channels.

**Important:** This playbook is educational guidance. Real containment actions require appropriate authorization and must follow the organization's incident response policy.
