DAY 11 — SOCIAL ENGINEERING AWARENESS TRAINING

**1. Objective**

To build an interactive Python quiz that teaches users to recognize social engineering tactics and choose safer responses.

**2. Topics Covered**

- Phishing links and password requests.
- Unknown USB devices and baiting.
- Unexpected requests for one-time authentication codes.
- Urgency and fear tactics.
- Suspicious attachments.
- Physical tailgating.
- Fake social media giveaways.
- Gift-card and payment scams.
- Untrusted browser extensions.
- Response to suspected credential compromise.

**3. Implementation**

The quiz presents ten scenario-based questions with three choices each. It validates answers, provides immediate feedback, displays explanations, calculates a score and percentage, and saves results as JSON.

**4. Test Results**

The test run scored 5 out of 10, equivalent to 50%. Five answers were correct and five were incorrect. The application completed all questions and saved `reports/quiz_results.json`.

**5. Defensive Recommendations**

- Verify unexpected requests through trusted channels.
- Never disclose passwords or one-time authentication codes.
- Do not connect unknown USB devices.
- Treat urgent financial requests with caution.
- Verify unexpected attachments and downloads before opening them.
- Follow physical access-control procedures.
- Report suspected phishing and impersonation promptly.
- Change compromised passwords through the legitimate service and review account security.

**6. Limitations**

This quiz is a basic educational tool. A score does not fully measure a person's security awareness, and the questions do not cover every possible social engineering scenario.

**7. Ethical Scope**

The exercise uses fictional scenarios and does not send real phishing messages, collect credentials, or target real users.

**8. Conclusion**

The quiz demonstrates how scenario-based training, immediate feedback, and score tracking can reinforce safer responses to social engineering threats.
